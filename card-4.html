Session 4: Revelation 8:6-10:11

For a participant: offer these when there is an opening, not as a lecture. Full session study

Parallel snippet, Rev 9:20

The Message 9:20
The remaining men and women who weren’t killed by these weapons went on their ...
NIV 9:20
The rest of mankind who were not killed by these plagues still did not ...
NRSVue 9:20
The rest of humankind, who were not killed by these plagues, did not repent ...

Evans: Evans would apply this to hardened hearts in the tribulation and urge response to the gospel now (inferred from his dispensational, kingdom-agenda framework).

Agree or differ: Strong agreement on the lesson: warnings do not change hearts by themselves.

Red marks the words of Jesus.

Insights to offer

1. Structure, a new exodus. The trumpets copy the Exodus plagues: hail and fire, water to blood, darkness, locusts. Like Pharaoh, the survivors still did not repent (9:20-21MSG | NIV | NRSVue).

2. Greek, 'no more delay'. In 10:6MSG | NIV | NRSVue chronos ouketi estai means 'there will be no more delay', answering the martyrs' 'How long?' (6:10MSG | NIV | NRSVue).

3. OT echo, the scroll. Eating the scroll (10:9-10MSG | NIV | NRSVue) repeats Ezek 3:1-3MSG | NIV | NRSVue: God's word is sweet to receive and bitter to carry.

4. Roman context, Parthians. The cavalry from beyond the Euphrates (9:14-16MSG | NIV | NRSVue) played on Rome's fear of the Parthians, the one army it never conquered.

Steering questions

  • The survivors 'did not repent' (9:20-21MSG | NIV | NRSVue) even after judgment. What patterns have you excused despite clear warning?
  • 9:21MSG | NIV | NRSVue lists murders, sorceries (pharmakeia), sexual immorality and theft. Which of these does our culture market to men most aggressively?
  • John eats the scroll: sweet, then bitter (10:9-10MSG | NIV | NRSVue). When has obeying God's word cost you something?
  • Why did judgment not lead to repentance (9:20-21MSG | NIV | NRSVue)? What turns the story toward witness in chapter 10MSG | NIV | NRSVue?

Bridging a tangent

  • On dates and headlines: 'Christians have read this differently for 2,000 years. What do all the views agree it asks of us?'
  • On a long debate: 'Whatever we decide about that, where does the text land for us this week?'
  • On a personal story: 'That connects to 9:20-21MSG | NIV | NRSVue. Can we look at it together?'
  • On fear or anxiety: 'Notice who is on the throne in this passage. It is written to steady us.'
  • Handing back: 'I'll stop there. [Leader], where did you want to go next?'

If someone asks

Are the trumpets literal? Futurists read literal future judgments; others read the imagery, modelled on the Exodus plagues, as symbolic of judgments that recur through history. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): literal future judgments following the seals.

Who are the locusts and the 200 million? Many read the locusts as demonic powers (they come from the abyss and have a king, Abaddon). Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): literal or demonic forces in the tribulation; the specific Asian-army link is not confirmed for him.

What is the little scroll? Some identify it with the Lamb's scroll of chapter 5MSG | NIV | NRSVue (Bauckham), now opened and given to John to proclaim; others see a separate commission.

New International Version NIV

(7:9) After this I looked, and there before me was a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people and language, standing before the throne and before the Lamb. They were wearing white robes and were holding palm branches in their hands.

NRSV Updated Edition NRSVue

(7:9) After this I looked, and there was a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, robed in white, with palm branches in their hands.

Greek: sphragis ('seal'); thlipsis megalē ('the great tribulation', 7:14); 'how long?' (heōs pote, 6:10).

OT allusions: Zech 1:8 and 6:1-8 (horses); Ezek 9:4 (sealing the faithful); Isa 34:4 and Joel 2:31 (cosmic signs); Isa 49:10 and 25:8 (7:16-17).

Roman context: 6:6 grain at famine prices while oil and wine are spared. Many link this to Domitian's vine edict of AD 92 (Suetonius, Domitian 7.2). The white horse (6:2) may evoke the Parthians, Rome's feared mounted archers.

Interpretive schools: Recapitulationists (e.g., Beale) read the seals as conditions spanning the whole church age. Dispensational futurists place them in the first half of a future seven-year tribulation. Bauckham argues the 144,000 (heard) and the countless multitude (seen) are the same group described in two ways (compare 5:5-6).

Conclusions: The interlude answers 6:17, 'who is able to stand?': the sealed people of God. The seventh seal's silence and the incense of prayer (8:1-5) show that the saints' prayers play a part in God's judgments.

Discussion questions

Men's group (Monday)

1. The martyrs ask 'How long?' (6:10) and are told to rest a little longer. Where are you waiting on God for justice?

2. In 6:15-17 kings, generals and the rich hide from the Lamb. Why does power make facing God harder?

3. The multitude of 7:9 is from every nation. What does that ask of our group's friendships and witness?

Co-ed group (Tuesday)

1. The martyrs' 'How long?' is a lament. How do we make room for lament in our group without rushing to fix it?

2. Chapter 7 shows God's people sealed and safe before the next judgments. Where have you felt that kind of protection?

3. 7:16-17 promises God will wipe away every tear. Who among us needs that promise this week?

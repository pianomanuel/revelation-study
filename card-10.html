Session 10: Revelation 22:6-21

For a participant: offer these when there is an opening, not as a lecture. Full session study

Parallel snippet, Rev 22:20

The Message 22:20
He who testifies to all these things says it again: “I’m on my way! ...
NIV 22:20
He who testifies to these things says, “Yes, I am coming soon.” Amen. Come, ...
NRSVue 22:20
The one who testifies to these things says, “Surely I am coming soon.” Amen. ...

Evans: Evans: the rapture could happen 'in the twinkling of an eye' (Evans, sermon 'God's Prophetic Calendar' (transcript, sermons.love)).

Agree or differ: Agree on imminence; differ on rapture before return.

Red marks the words of Jesus.

Insights to offer

1. Greek, 'Come'. Erchou, 'Come!', runs both ways: the Spirit and the bride invite the thirsty to come (22:17MSG | NIV | NRSVue), and the church prays 'Come, Lord Jesus' (22:20MSG | NIV | NRSVue), the Aramaic Marana tha (1 Cor 16:22MSG | NIV | NRSVue).

2. OT echo, First and Last. In 22:13MSG | NIV | NRSVue Jesus takes God's own titles from Isa 44:6MSG | NIV | NRSVue and 48:12.

3. Structure, the frame. The book ends as it began (1:3MSG | NIV | NRSVue): a beatitude, a warning and grace. These are words to keep, not just decode.

4. Text, 'book of life'. The KJV's 'book of life' in 22:19MSG | NIV | NRSVue comes from Erasmus translating Latin back into Greek; every Greek manuscript reads 'tree of life'.

Steering questions

  • Jesus says 'I am coming soon' three times. How would you live this week if you took that seriously?
  • 'Let everyone who is thirsty come' (22:17MSG | NIV | NRSVue). Which man in your life needs that invitation?
  • 22:18-19MSG | NIV | NRSVue warns against adding to or taking away from the prophecy. How do we handle speculation about the end responsibly?
  • How do we pray 'Come, Lord Jesus' and invite the thirsty at the same time?

Bridging a tangent

  • On dates and headlines: 'Christians have read this differently for 2,000 years. What do all the views agree it asks of us?'
  • On a long debate: 'Whatever we decide about that, where does the text land for us this week?'
  • On a personal story: 'That connects to 22:17MSG | NIV | NRSVue. Can we look at it together?'
  • On fear or anxiety: 'Notice who is on the throne in this passage. It is written to steady us.'
  • Handing back: 'I'll stop there. [Leader], where did you want to go next?'

If someone asks

What does 'soon' mean if 2,000 years have passed? Answers include imminence (it could happen at any time), prophetic perspective (God's time, 2 Pet 3:8MSG | NIV | NRSVue), first-century fulfilment of the judgments (preterism), and the 'already' of the kingdom inaugurated at the cross. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): imminence: Christ's return could happen at any moment.

Does 22:18-19 apply to the whole Bible? Strictly it guards 'this book', echoing Deut 4:2MSG | NIV | NRSVue.

Why does the KJV say 'book of life' in 22:19? Erasmus's only Greek manuscript lacked the last verses, so he back-translated from a Latin text that read libro ('book') for ligno ('tree').

Session 8: Revelation 19:6-20:15

For a participant: offer these when there is an opening, not as a lecture. Full session study

Parallel snippet, Rev 19:16

The Message 19:16
A White Horse and Its RiderThen I saw Heaven open wide—and oh! a white ...
NIV 19:16
On his robe and on his thigh he has this name written: king of ...
NRSVue 19:16
On his robe and on his thigh he has a name inscribed, “King of ...

Evans: Evans: 'The Word of God will be a sword in the mouth of Christ ... Jesus need only say the word to crush any rebellion' (Evans, 'Christ's Millennial Rule' (tonyevans.org blog)).

Agree or differ: Agree that his word wins the victory.

Red marks the words of Jesus.

Insights to offer

1. Greek, 'dipped in blood'. The rider's robe is already 'dipped in blood' (19:13MSG | NIV | NRSVue) before the battle. Many see his own blood.

2. Structure, the sword. The enemies fall by 'the sword coming out of his mouth' (19:15MSG | NIV | NRSVue, 21): the victory is won by his word, and no battle is actually described.

3. OT echo, Gog and Magog. 20:8MSG | NIV | NRSVue draws on Ezek 38-39MSG | NIV | NRSVue. 19:17-21MSG | NIV | NRSVue and 20:7-10MSG | NIV | NRSVue both quote it, which amillennialists take as two views of one final battle.

4. Roman context, titles. 'King of kings and Lord of lords' (19:16MSG | NIV | NRSVue) contested imperial titles. Roman triumphs paraded a victor on a white horse.

Steering questions

  • Christ's robe is dipped in blood before the battle (19:13MSG | NIV | NRSVue) and he wins by the sword from his mouth. What does that say about how Christian men fight?
  • Judgment is 'according to their works' (20:12MSG | NIV | NRSVue), yet the book of life decides (20:15MSG | NIV | NRSVue). How do you hold both?
  • Pre-, a- and postmillennial readings of 20:1-6MSG | NIV | NRSVue differ. Does your view change how you live on Monday morning?
  • Whatever our millennial view, what does the rider's victory by his word and his bloodied robe teach?

Bridging a tangent

  • On dates and headlines: 'Christians have read this differently for 2,000 years. What do all the views agree it asks of us?'
  • On a long debate: 'Whatever we decide about that, where does the text land for us this week?'
  • On a personal story: 'That connects to 19:13MSG | NIV | NRSVue. Can we look at it together?'
  • On fear or anxiety: 'Notice who is on the throne in this passage. It is written to steady us.'
  • Handing back: 'I'll stop there. [Leader], where did you want to go next?'

If someone asks

Is the millennium literal? Premillennialists (including historic premillennialists like Ladd and Mounce) expect a reign on earth after Christ's return. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): a literal thousand-year earthly reign from Jerusalem, fulfilling promises to Israel.

Is Satan bound now? Amillennialists connect 20:2-3MSG | NIV | NRSVue with Jesus binding the strong man (Mark 3:27MSG | NIV | NRSVue) so the nations can hear the gospel. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): a future binding at Christ's return.

Whose blood is on the rider's robe (19:13)? The robe is bloody before any battle.

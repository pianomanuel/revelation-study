Revelation 21:1-22:5

The Message MSG

(21:3-5) I heard a voice thunder from the Throne: “Look! Look! God has moved into the neighborhood, making his home with men and women! They’re his people, he’s their God. He’ll wipe every tear from their eyes. Death is gone for good—tears gone, crying gone, pain gone—all the first order of things gone.” The Enthroned continued, “Look! I’m making everything new. Write it all down—each word dependable and accurate.”

New International Version NIV

(21:3) And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, “Look! God’s dwelling place is now among the people, and he will dwell with them. They will be his people, and God himself will be with them and be their God.

NRSV Updated Edition NRSVue

(21:3) And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, “See, the home of God is among mortals. He will dwell with them; they will be his peoples, and God himself will be with them and be their God;

Greek: kainos ('new' in quality, 21:1, 5); skēnē/skēnōsei ('tabernacle', 'will dwell', 21:3; compare John 1:14); xylon zōēs ('tree of life').

OT allusions: Isa 65:17 and 66:22 (new heavens and earth); Ezek 37:27 and Lev 26:11-12 (covenant formula, 21:3); Isa 25:8 (tears); Ezek 40-48 (city and river, Ezek 47); Isa 60 (nations bringing glory); Gen 2-3 (paradise restored).

Roman context: The bride-city replaces the prostitute-city. Its open gates and nations bringing glory (21:24-26) reverse Babylon's extraction of wealth.

Interpretive schools: Futurists, including dispensationalists, read a literal new creation after the millennium. Some dispensationalists also expect a literal cubic city. Most scholars read the cube (21:16) as the Holy of Holies (1 Kgs 6:20) enlarged to the size of the whole city: all God's people dwell in God's presence. Debate continues over renewal versus replacement of the present creation.

Conclusions: The goal of history is God dwelling with his people on a renewed earth, not escape to heaven. There is no temple 'for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb' (21:22).

Discussion questions

Men's group (Monday)

1. 21:4 promises no more tears. What loss are you carrying that this verse speaks to?

2. There is no temple because God and the Lamb are its temple (21:22). How does God's presence change your view of ordinary work?

3. 'Nothing unclean will enter' (21:27). How does that shape the way you fight temptation now?

Co-ed group (Tuesday)

1. 21:3-5 is often read at funerals. Which promise here comforts you most, and why?

2. The city's gates never close and the nations bring their glory in (21:25-26). What does that say about God's welcome?

3. The tree of life's leaves are 'for the healing of the nations' (22:2). Where do you long to see healing?

Session 2: Revelation 4-5

For a participant: offer these when there is an opening, not as a lecture. Full session study

Parallel snippet, Rev 5:5

The Message 5:5
I wept and wept and wept that no one was found able to open ...
NIV 5:5
Then one of the elders said to me, “Do not weep! See, the Lion ...
NRSVue 5:5
Then one of the elders said to me, “Do not weep. See, the Lion ...

Evans: Evans would stress that the Lamb alone is worthy to take the 'title deed' to the earth and begin the judgments of the tribulation (inferred from his dispensational, kingdom-agenda framework).

Agree or differ: Agree on Christ's worthiness; scholars stress the reinterpretation of power more than a future transfer of title.

Red marks the words of Jesus.

Insights to offer

1. Structure, Lion and Lamb. John hears 'the Lion of the tribe of Judah' (5:5MSG | NIV | NRSVue) but turns and sees a Lamb standing as though slaughtered (5:6MSG | NIV | NRSVue). Victory is redefined by the cross, Bauckham's key to the book.

2. Greek, 'worthy'. Axios ei, 'you are worthy' (4:11MSG | NIV | NRSVue; 5:9MSG | NIV | NRSVue), was the acclamation for an emperor's arrival. John gives it only to God and the Lamb.

3. OT echo, the throne. Chapter 4 fuses Ezek 1MSG | NIV | NRSVue (creatures, rainbow) and Isa 6MSG | NIV | NRSVue ('Holy, holy, holy'). The sealed scroll answers Dan 12:4MSG | NIV | NRSVue, where Daniel was told to seal the book.

4. Roman context, crowns. Client kings laid crowns before the emperor (Tiridates before Nero). The elders casting their crowns (4:10MSG | NIV | NRSVue) is a picture of where true authority lies.

Steering questions

  • The elders cast their crowns before the throne (4:10MSG | NIV | NRSVue). What 'crowns' (titles, achievements, provider identity) do men find hardest to lay down?
  • John hears 'Lion' and sees a slaughtered Lamb (5:5-6MSG | NIV | NRSVue). How does that redefine strength and leadership for a man?
  • How does 4:11MSG | NIV | NRSVue (God worthy as Creator) speak to a man's sense of control over his work and future?
  • Why does John see a Lamb after hearing about a Lion? What changes if worship is how we read history?

Bridging a tangent

  • On dates and headlines: 'Christians have read this differently for 2,000 years. What do all the views agree it asks of us?'
  • On a long debate: 'Whatever we decide about that, where does the text land for us this week?'
  • On a personal story: 'That connects to 5:5-6MSG | NIV | NRSVue. Can we look at it together?'
  • On fear or anxiety: 'Notice who is on the throne in this passage. It is written to steady us.'
  • Handing back: 'I'll stop there. [Leader], where did you want to go next?'

If someone asks

Who are the 24 elders? Main views: glorified representatives of God's people (12 tribes plus 12 apostles, compare 21:12-14MSG | NIV | NRSVue), an angelic council of heaven's court (Isa 24:23MSG | NIV | NRSVue), or a symbol of the whole church. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): the elders represent the raptured church, crowned in heaven before the tribulation.

Is 'Come up here' (4:1) the rapture? Dispensationalists often read John's summons as a picture of the church's rapture, noting that the word 'church' does not recur until 22:16MSG | NIV | NRSVue. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): typically treats 4:1MSG | NIV | NRSVue as a picture of the rapture.

What is the sealed scroll? Suggestions include a Roman will sealed by seven witnesses, a title deed (Jer 32MSG | NIV | NRSVue), Ezekiel's scroll of lament (Ezek 2:9-10MSG | NIV | NRSVue) or Daniel's sealed book (Dan 12:4MSG | NIV | NRSVue). Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): often described as the title deed to the earth.

Greek terms

kalamos (11:1): 'Measuring reed'. Measuring signifies preservation and ownership (Ezek 40-42MSG | NIV | NRSVue; Zech 2:1-5MSG | NIV | NRSVue): the inner sanctuary is protected while the outer court is trampled.

naos (11:1, 19): The sanctuary proper, not the whole temple complex (hieron). John uses naos for God's dwelling, and in 3:12MSG | NIV | NRSVue believers become pillars in it.

dyo martyres (11:3): 'Two witnesses'. Two is the legal minimum for valid testimony (Deut 19:15MSG | NIV | NRSVue).

42 months / 1,260 days / 'time, times and half a time' (11:2-3; 12:6, 14): All equal three and a half years, from Dan 7:25MSG | NIV | NRSVue and 12:7MSG | NIV | NRSVue. The period of the church's suffering witness, read literally by futurists and symbolically by others.

ptōma (11:8-9): 'Corpse', singular, for two bodies. Some take this to mean the witnesses represent one corporate witness.

pneumatikōs (11:8): 'Spiritually' or 'prophetically'. The city is 'spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified'. John signals a non-literal, typological identification.

egeneto hē basileia tou kosmou (11:15): 'The kingdom of the world has become...'. The aorist is proleptic: the outcome is so certain it is spoken of as done. 'Kingdom' here is singular.

drakōn, ophis ho archaios, diabolos, satanas (12:9): Four titles for one enemy: dragon, ancient serpent, devil (slanderer), Satan (adversary).

katēgōr (12:10): 'Accuser'. A Greek transliteration of a Hebrew/Aramaic legal term, used of Satan in Job 1-2MSG | NIV | NRSVue and Zech 3MSG | NIV | NRSVue.

enikēsan (12:11): 'They conquered'. The church's victory is described in the same terms as the Lamb's (5:5MSG | NIV | NRSVue), and 'did not love their lives unto death' answers the overcomer promises of chapters 2-3MSG | NIV | NRSVue.

Roman context

Chapter 12 retells a combat myth widely known in the Greco-Roman world: Leto, pregnant with Apollo, is pursued by the dragon Python. She escapes to an island and Apollo later kills the dragon. Coins of Asia Minor depicted Leto's flight, and imperial propaganda cast emperors (notably Nero) as the new Apollo. Aune and Koester discuss this background, and Bauckham notes the subversion: John recasts the story so that the true divine child is Christ and the dragon stands behind the very imperial power that claimed Apollo's role. The 'great city' of 11:8MSG | NIV | NRSVue is debated: Jerusalem ('where their Lord was crucified') or Rome/the world-city (the term elsewhere means Babylon/Rome, e.g., 16:19MSG | NIV | NRSVue; 17:18MSG | NIV | NRSVue).

Interpretive schools

Dispensational futurist (Evans; inferred from his published dispensational premillennial stance, not quoted from the videos): Two literal individual prophets, often identified as Moses and Elijah or Enoch and Elijah, ministering in Jerusalem during the first half of the tribulation in a rebuilt temple. The woman of chapter 12MSG | NIV | NRSVue is ethnic Israel, protected in the wilderness during the 'great tribulation' (the second three and a half years). The 'war in heaven' marks Satan's expulsion at the tribulation midpoint. Check Evans's video for his specific identifications.

Preterist: The temple is the Jerusalem temple before AD 70, which supports an early date. The two witnesses may be Peter and Paul or the Christian witness in Jerusalem.

Historicist: The witnesses are the Scriptures (Old and New Testaments) or the faithful Protestant witness through the Middle Ages.

Idealist and modified idealist (Beale, Bauckham): The two witnesses are the whole witnessing church. They are explicitly 'the two lampstands', and 1:20MSG | NIV | NRSVue identifies lampstands as churches. Two of the seven churches (Smyrna and Philadelphia) received no rebuke. The woman is the messianic community: Israel bearing Messiah, then the church. The dragon's expulsion is tied to Christ's death and exaltation (12:5MSG | NIV | NRSVue, 10-11: 'by the blood of the Lamb'), not to a future date. Mounce and Osborne land between these positions. Osborne, for example, sees the witnesses as two future individuals who also symbolize the church's witness.

Textual notes

11:17MSG | NIV | NRSVue: NA28 reads 'who are and who were', without the Textus Receptus addition 'and who are to come'. The omission is significant: after the seventh trumpet God has 'begun to reign', so the 'coming' has arrived (compare 16:5MSG | NIV | NRSVue). 12:18MSG | NIV | NRSVue (NRSVue and NA28: 'Then the dragon took his stand on the sand of the seashore') is 13:1MSG | NIV | NRSVuea in some Bibles. The Textus Receptus, followed by the KJV, has 'I stood' (estathēn) rather than 'he stood' (estathē), so the verse numbering differs between translations. 11:12MSG | NIV | NRSVue: the manuscripts are divided between 'they heard' and 'I heard'.

Conclusions

Revelation 11-12MSG | NIV | NRSVue is the book's central theological statement. Chapter 11 answers what the plagues could not achieve: the nations repent when God's people bear faithful, suffering, Spirit-empowered witness that follows the pattern of the Lamb (death, vindication, ascension). The seventh trumpet proclaims the kingdom as already secured. Chapter 12 explains the church's experience: the dragon has already been defeated through the cross and expelled. His rage on earth is the fury of a beaten foe whose 'time is short', and believers conquer as the Lamb did. Whatever the eschatological timetable, the pastoral word is identical across the schools: witness, endure, and do not fear the accuser.

If asked

Who are the two witnesses? Views: two literal end-time prophets (often Moses and Elijah, or Enoch and Elijah); Peter and Paul (some preterists); or the whole witnessing church, since they are called 'the two lampstands' and lampstands are churches (1:20MSG | NIV | NRSVue). Their powers echo Moses and Elijah. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): two literal prophets in Jerusalem during the tribulation.

Who is the woman of chapter 12? Main readings: Israel (Gen 37:9MSG | NIV | NRSVue, sun, moon and stars; the twelve tribes); the people of God as one community, Israel bearing the Messiah and then the church; Mary, in later Catholic devotion. Most scholars read her primarily as the messianic community. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): national Israel, protected during the tribulation.

Is the 1,260 days literal? The same period appears as 42 months and 'time, times and half a time' (Dan 7:25MSG | NIV | NRSVue), all three and a half years: a limited time of persecution. Futurists read a literal half of the tribulation; historicists used the day-year principle (1,260 years); idealists read the whole church age as a limited time. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): literal days in the tribulation.

Steering questions: How do the witnesses conquer (11:7-12MSG | NIV | NRSVue; 12:11MSG | NIV | NRSVue)? Where does that pattern show up in our lives?

Discussion questions

Men's group (Monday)

1. The two witnesses are killed and the world celebrates (11:10MSG | NIV | NRSVue). How do you handle being mocked for faithfulness?

2. 12:11MSG | NIV | NRSVue: the blood of the Lamb, the word of testimony, and not clinging to life. Which of the three is weakest in you?

3. The dragon is furious 'because he knows his time is short' (12:12MSG | NIV | NRSVue). How does that change how you read spiritual attack?

Co-ed group (Tuesday)

1. The two witnesses are 'lampstands', a symbol of the church (11:4MSG | NIV | NRSVue; 1:20MSG | NIV | NRSVue). How is our group a witness together, not only as individuals?

2. 12:11MSG | NIV | NRSVue says believers conquer by the Lamb's blood and their testimony. Would anyone share a short testimony of God's faithfulness?

3. Chapter 12 reveals an unseen battle behind visible struggles. How should that change the way we respond to each other in conflict?

Session 1: Revelation 1-3

For a participant: offer these when there is an opening, not as a lecture. Full session study

Parallel snippet, Rev 1:17

The Message 1:17
I, John, with you all the way in the trial and the Kingdom and ...
NIV 1:17
When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. Then he ...
NRSVue 1:17
When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. But he ...

Evans: Evans would read this as the King establishing his authority before the prophetic program unfolds (inferred from his dispensational, kingdom-agenda framework).

Agree or differ: Agree on the high Christology; scholars add the first-century martyr setting more strongly.

Red marks the words of Jesus.

Insights to offer

1. Greek, 'is coming'. In 1:4MSG | NIV | NRSVue and 1:8MSG | NIV | NRSVue God is 'who is and who was and who is coming'. John breaks Greek grammar to keep the divine name unchanged (compare Exod 3:14MSG | NIV | NRSVue), and the third slot is not 'will be' but 'is coming'.

2. OT echo, Daniel 7. The figure in 1:13-16MSG | NIV | NRSVue is the 'son of man' of Dan 7:13MSG | NIV | NRSVue, but with the white hair of the Ancient of Days (Dan 7:9MSG | NIV | NRSVue). John places Jesus within God's own identity.

3. Roman context, Laodicea. Laodicea rebuilt after the AD 60 earthquake without imperial aid, so 'I am rich; I need nothing' (3:17MSG | NIV | NRSVue) was civic pride. Its tepid piped water (Hierapolis hot, Colossae cold) makes 'lukewarm' mean useless, not half-hearted.

4. Structure, the promises. Every promise to 'the one who conquers' in chapters 2-3MSG | NIV | NRSVue is fulfilled in chapters 21-22MSG | NIV | NRSVue. The letters set the stakes for the whole book.

Steering questions

  • Laodicea's self-assessment was 'I need nothing' (3:17MSG | NIV | NRSVue). Where does financial or professional self-sufficiency make men spiritually tepid?
  • Pergamum and Thyatira faced trade-guild pressure, where faithfulness cost income. What are the equivalent compromises in our workplaces?
  • Evans, as a futurist, and Beale, as an idealist, read 3:10MSG | NIV | NRSVue differently. Does your view of the end change how you endure now?
  • What in Christ's self-description (1:12-18MSG | NIV | NRSVue) speaks to our situation? Which letter sounds most like our church?

Bridging a tangent

  • On dates and headlines: 'Christians have read this differently for 2,000 years. What do all the views agree it asks of us?'
  • On a long debate: 'Whatever we decide about that, where does the text land for us this week?'
  • On a personal story: 'That connects to 1:17-18MSG | NIV | NRSVue. Can we look at it together?'
  • On fear or anxiety: 'Notice who is on the throne in this passage. It is written to steady us.'
  • Handing back: 'I'll stop there. [Leader], where did you want to go next?'

If someone asks

Are the seven churches seven eras of church history? The letters were addressed to seven real congregations in Asia; the number seven also signals the whole church. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): would treat the churches as real and applicable today; whether he uses the church-age scheme is not confirmed.

Does 3:10 teach a rapture before the tribulation? 'I will keep you from (tērein ek) the hour of trial' is a standard pretribulation proof text, read as removal from the period. Evans (inferred from his dispensational stance): pretribulational: the church is removed before the tribulation.

What does 'lukewarm' mean? Rudwick and Green's reading of Laodicea's water supply is widely followed: Hierapolis had healing hot springs, Colossae cold fresh water, and Laodicea tepid, mineral-laden water piped in.

Revelation 19:6-20:15

Watch on RightNow Media · Read the chapter in the Bible App: MSG NIV NRSVue

The Message MSG

[Rev 19:11-16: MSG renders this as one paragraph; not quoted here to limit quotation. Read it in The Message.]

New International Version NIV

(19:16) On his robe and on his thigh he has this name written: king of kings and lord of lords.

NRSV Updated Edition NRSVue

(19:16) On his robe and on his thigh he has a name inscribed, “King of kings and Lord of lords.”

Greek: gamos tou arniou ('marriage of the Lamb'); chilia etē ('a thousand years'); hē anastasis hē prōtē ('the first resurrection', 20:5).

OT allusions: Ps 2:9 (iron rod); Isa 63:1-6 (robe dipped in blood); Isa 11:4 (sword of his mouth); Ezek 38-39 (Gog and Magog); Dan 7:9-10 (books opened).

Roman context: 'King of kings and Lord of lords' (19:16) directly challenges imperial titulature.

Interpretive schools: Premillennialists (Evans, Mounce, Osborne) read 20:1-6 as following Christ's return chronologically: a literal or extended reign on earth. Dispensationalists add a restored Israel. Amillennialists (Beale) read 20:1-6 as recapitulating the church age, with 'binding' fulfilled at the cross (compare Mark 3:27). Postmillennialists expect a gospel-shaped golden age. Note that the rider's robe is bloodied before the battle (19:13), which may be his own blood.

Conclusions: The returning Christ conquers 'with the sword of his mouth' (19:15, 21), his word. The final judgment is according to works, but the deciding factor is the Lamb's book of life (20:12-15).

Discussion questions

Men's group (Monday)

1. Christ's robe is dipped in blood before the battle (19:13) and he wins by the sword from his mouth. What does that say about how Christian men fight?

2. Judgment is 'according to their works' (20:12), yet the book of life decides (20:15). How do you hold both?

3. Pre-, a- and postmillennial readings of 20:1-6 differ. Does your view change how you live on Monday morning?

Co-ed group (Tuesday)

1. The marriage supper of the Lamb (19:7-9) uses wedding imagery. What does it teach about Christ's love for the church?

2. Judgment by works (20:12) versus the book of life (20:15): how do grace and accountability fit together?

3. How do the different millennial views affect the hope we share with our children and friends?

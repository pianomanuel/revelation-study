Greek terms

hoi gamoi tou arniou (19:7): 'The marriage of the Lamb'. The bride is clothed in 'fine linen, bright and pure', defined as 'the righteous deeds of the saints' (19:8), given to her to wear.

pistos kai alēthinos (19:11): 'Faithful and True'. The rider's name echoes 3:14; he 'judges and makes war in righteousness'.

himation bebammenon haimati (19:13): 'A robe dipped in blood'. Before any battle is described. Many (Caird, Bauckham, Beale) read it as his own blood or the martyrs'; others as enemy blood from Isa 63.

ho logos tou theou (19:13): 'The Word of God'. The only use of the title outside the Johannine Gospel prologue and 1 John.

rhomphaia oxeia (19:15, 21): 'A sharp sword' coming from his mouth. The enemies are killed by the word he speaks, not by a weapon in his hand.

chilia etē (20:2-7): 'A thousand years'. Used six times. The only explicit reference in Scripture to a thousand-year reign.

edēsen (20:2): 'He bound'. Compare Mark 3:27, binding the strong man. The binding concerns deceiving 'the nations' (20:3).

ezēsan (20:4): 'They came to life'. Premillennialists read physical resurrection; amillennialists read the souls of martyrs living and reigning with Christ now.

hē anastasis hē prōtē (20:5-6): 'The first resurrection'. The fifth beatitude: 'blessed and holy are those who share in the first resurrection'. The 'second death' (20:6, 14) is the lake of fire.

Old Testament allusions

The wedding imagery draws on Isa 54:5-6, 61:10, 62:5 and Hos 2:19-20. The rider's portrait combines Ps 2:9 (iron rod), Isa 11:4 (the breath of his lips slaying the wicked), Isa 49:2 (a mouth like a sharp sword) and Isa 63:1-3 (the winepress and robes stained red). The birds summoned to the 'great supper of God' (19:17-18) come from Ezek 39:17-20, the aftermath of Gog's defeat. Gog and Magog (20:8) themselves come from Ezek 38-39, where Gog of the land of Magog attacks a restored Israel. 'The camp of the saints and the beloved city' (20:9) evokes Israel in the wilderness and Jerusalem. The thrones and books of 20:4, 12 draw on Dan 7:9-10, 22 (judgment given to the saints) and Dan 12:1-2 (the book; resurrection to life and to shame). Fire from heaven (20:9) recalls 2 Kgs 1:10-12 and Ezek 38:22.

Roman context

Roman triumphs paraded a victorious general on a white horse through the city, followed by captives and spoils. Revelation's rider wins a triumph without a battle being fought. 'King of kings and Lord of lords' (19:16; compare 17:14) challenges titles claimed by emperors and eastern monarchs. The thrones of judgment answer Roman courts that condemned Christians 'for the testimony of Jesus' (20:4); beheading (pelekizō, 'by the axe') was the punishment for Roman citizens, as tradition holds for Paul. Ancient conventions of the 'thousand-year' age appear in Jewish texts (2 Enoch 33; compare 4 Ezra 7:28, which has a 400-year messianic kingdom).

Interpretive schools

Dispensational futurist (Evans; inferred from his published dispensational premillennial stance, not quoted from the videos): Chapter 19 describes Christ's literal second coming to earth at the end of the tribulation. Chapter 20 follows chronologically: Satan is bound and Christ reigns for a literal thousand years on earth from Jerusalem, fulfilling God's promises to national Israel, with resurrected and raptured believers reigning with him. A final rebellion and the great white throne judgment of unbelievers follow. This is premillennialism in its dispensational form.

Historic premillennial (Ladd, Mounce, Osborne): Christ returns before an earthly millennium, but without a pretribulation rapture or a sharp Israel-church distinction.

Amillennial (Augustine, Beale, Hoekema): 20:1-6 recapitulates and portrays the whole church age from heaven's perspective. Satan was bound at the cross so that he cannot deceive the nations from receiving the gospel; the martyrs reign with Christ now; the 'first resurrection' is either regeneration or the soul's entry into Christ's presence at death. 20:7-10 and 19:17-21 describe the same final battle (both quote Ezek 38-39).

Postmillennial (Edwards, Warfield): The gospel will bring a long era of righteousness and peace before Christ's return.

Preterist: The rider's victory is the triumph of the gospel over Rome and Jerusalem; the millennium is the church age.

Textual notes

19:13: 'dipped' (bebammenon, A, 046) is the most likely reading; variants 'sprinkled' (rerantismenon, rerammenon, from Isa 63:3 LXX) show early scribes trying to interpret the image. 20:5a ('The rest of the dead did not come to life until the thousand years were ended') is missing from Aleph and some later manuscripts but is well supported by A and 046 and is accepted as original. 20:12: the Textus Receptus 'before God' versus the earliest reading 'before the throne'.

Conclusions

Christ's victory is certain and is won by his word, the sword from his mouth, and his robe is bloody before any fighting is described. The millennium is debated more than any passage in Revelation, and good-faith scholars differ. Every school agrees on what the text emphasizes: the vindication of the martyrs who refused the beast, the defeat of Satan, and a final judgment in which works matter, yet the deciding question is whose names are in the Lamb's book of life. The second death has no power over those who share in the first resurrection.

Discussion questions

Men's group (Monday)

1. Christ's robe is dipped in blood before the battle (19:13) and he wins by the sword from his mouth. What does that say about how Christian men fight?

2. Judgment is 'according to their works' (20:12), yet the book of life decides (20:15). How do you hold both?

3. Pre-, a- and postmillennial readings of 20:1-6 differ. Does your view change how you live on Monday morning?

Co-ed group (Tuesday)

1. The marriage supper of the Lamb (19:7-9) uses wedding imagery. What does it teach about Christ's love for the church?

2. Judgment by works (20:12) versus the book of life (20:15): how do grace and accountability fit together?

3. How do the different millennial views affect the hope we share with our children and friends?

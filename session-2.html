New International Version NIV

(4:11) “You are worthy, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power, for you created all things, and by your will they were created and have their being.” (5:6) Then I saw a Lamb, looking as if it had been slain, standing at the center of the throne, encircled by the four living creatures and the elders. The Lamb had seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth.

NRSV Updated Edition NRSVue

(4:11) “You are worthy, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power, for you created all things, and by your will they existed and were created.” (5:6) Then I saw between the throne and the four living creatures and among the elders a Lamb standing as if it had been slaughtered, with seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth.

Greek: axios ('worthy', 4:11; 5:9, 12); arnion esphagmenon ('a Lamb standing as though slaughtered', 5:6). The perfect participle implies a lasting state: the Lamb is still marked by his death.

OT allusions: Ezek 1 and Isa 6 (living creatures, the trisagion); Dan 7:9-10 (throne and books); Gen 49:9 and Isa 11:1, 10 (Lion and Root); Isa 53:7 (Lamb).

Roman context: Throne-room liturgy echoes, and implicitly parodies, imperial court ceremony. The elders 'cast their crowns', as client kings did before an emperor.

Interpretive schools: Bauckham's 'Lion/Lamb' reversal: John hears 'Lion' and sees a Lamb (5:5-6). The messianic conquest happens through the cross. Dispensationalists often take the elders to represent the raptured church (4:1, 'come up here'). Most commentators read them as an angelic council or as symbolic representatives of all God's people.

Conclusions: Chapters 4-5 are the hinge of the book. Everything that follows unfolds from the throne and the slain Lamb. History is governed by worship, not by Rome.

Discussion questions

Men's group (Monday)

1. The elders cast their crowns before the throne (4:10). What 'crowns' (titles, achievements, provider identity) do men find hardest to lay down?

2. John hears 'Lion' and sees a slaughtered Lamb (5:5-6). How does that redefine strength and leadership for a man?

3. How does 4:11 (God worthy as Creator) speak to a man's sense of control over his work and future?

Co-ed group (Tuesday)

1. Which line of the throne-room worship (4:8, 4:11, 5:9-10, 5:12) would you most want to sing this week, and why?

2. 5:9-10 says Christ ransomed people 'from every tribe and language'. How does our group reflect that, and how could it more?

3. How does the Lion-who-is-a-Lamb image shape how we use influence in our homes and friendships?
